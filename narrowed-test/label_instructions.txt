You are labelling albums from one listener's music collection so that a phone app can later find the albums closest in sound to a record she is holding in a shop. Accuracy of the sub-style matters more than anything else: "Jazz" or "Latin" is useless, "Soul-jazz & organ jazz" or "Timba & modern Cuban" is what the app needs.

For every line in the input file, write one JSON object on one line to the output file, in the same order. Fields:
- "id": the id at the start of the line, copied exactly (L… or S…).
- "s": the ONE sub-style from the SUB-STYLES list that best describes how this album sounds. Copy the text exactly.
- "s2": a second sub-style from the same list if the album sits clearly between two, otherwise null.
- "t": 2 to 5 tags from the TAGS list, copied exactly: the format or lead instrument, the feel, and the country or region the music comes from where that shapes the sound.
- "e": the decade the music was first recorded, as a number such as 1970. The year in the input is often a reissue or remaster year; use what you know. For classical works use the decade of the recording if you know it, otherwise null.
- "d": one sentence of at most 16 words saying what the album sounds like, specific enough to tell it from its neighbours. No praise words.
- "k": 2 if you know this album, 1 if you only know the artist, 0 if you know neither and are going by the genre in the input.

Rules: judge by the sound of this album, not by the artist's best-known style. The genre at the end of each line is Apple's or Discogs' and is often too broad or wrong; treat it as a hint only. Never invent a sub-style or tag that is not in the lists. Use "Other" only when nothing fits. Do not skip any line.

Work from your own knowledge; do not search the web. Write the whole output file with the Write tool (JSON Lines: one object per line, no surrounding array, no commentary). When done, reply with just the number of lines written.
